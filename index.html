A data story · World Happiness Report 2023

The Longevity–Happiness
Gap

The countries that live longest aren't the ones that live happiest. What separates them isn't years — it's something we rarely measure.

Scroll to begin
01 — The Rule

Longer lives track with happier ones.

Across 136 countries, healthy life expectancy and happiness rise together — a correlation of r = 0.74. Richer, healthier societies tend to be happier societies.

That's the received wisdom. And it's mostly right.

02 — The Rule Holds

And it has held for fifteen years.

This isn't a fluke of one survey. From 2006 to 2022, year after year, the correlation between how long people live and how happy they are barely moves — it stays around r = 0.7.

A rule this stable feels like a law of nature. Watch what happens when we look closer.

03 — The Break

But look who breaks the rule.

Draw the trend line and the outliers scream. Hong Kong has the 3rd-longest healthy lifespan on Earth — yet sits two full points below the happiness its longevity predicts.

Meanwhile Mozambique and Mexico, with far shorter lives, are happier than their lifespans should allow.

The distance from the line is the gap.

04 — The Map of the Gap

The gap has a geography.

Paint every country by its gap and the world splits. Latin America glows blue — mid-length lives, outsized joy. East Asia and the Middle East run red: long lives, muted happiness.

Watch it breathe across the years — the pattern is stubborn, not random.

05 — The Reveal

Same lifespan. Different lives.

Split the 25 biggest over-performers from the 25 biggest under-performers. Their average healthy lifespan is nearly identical — 64.8 yrs vs 64.2 yrs.

Same years on the clock. Vastly different joy. So what explains it?

06 — The Great Decoupling

For 32 countries, the two are pulling apart.

Here is the finding almost no one expects. In 32 countries, healthy lifespan climbed by more than a year per decade — while happiness fell.

India added 3.4 years of healthy life per decade and lost a full point of happiness. Botswana, Jordan, Egypt — the same story.

More years. Less joy. The rule doesn't just bend — it breaks.

07 — The Why

Freedom and connection — not years.

It isn't money and it isn't lifespan. The gap is explained by two things: freedom to make life choices (r = +0.53) and social support (r = +0.45).

Over-performers average 0.84 freedom and 0.85 support — roughly 20% higher on each than the under-performers.

08 — Joy, or the Absence of Pain?

Longer lives buy less suffering — not more joy.

Split happiness into its two halves. Longevity is tied to less negative emotion (r = −0.49) far more than to more positive emotion (r = +0.20).

Extra years take the edge off the bad days. They don't add good ones.

09 — The Shock

The pandemic widened the gap.

2019 to 2021 didn't hit everyone the same. East Asia and Central & Eastern Europe actually rose, while South Asia and Latin America fell hardest.

A global shock, felt in local ways — the gap is cultural, not just economic.

10 — Your Country, Over Time

Trace any nation's path.

Where does a country sit today — and how did its gap change since 2006?

A good life isn't measured in years alone.

The world's happiest countries aren't the longest-lived. They're the ones where people feel free to choose and connected to others — measured not in the length of a life, but in the freedom to live it on your own terms, surrounded by people who have your back.

Sources & Methods

Data

  • World Happiness Report 2023 — happiness (life-ladder) and its six factors. worldhappiness.report
  • WHR longitudinal panel, 2006–2022 — 159 countries, 2,155 country-years, incl. positive/negative affect. Gallup World Poll via WHR
  • Our World in Data — healthy life expectancy (UN WPP) and GDP per capita (World Bank). ourworldindata.org

Method

  • The gap is the OLS residual of happiness regressed on healthy life expectancy — actual happiness minus the happiness a country's lifespan predicts.
  • The choropleth refits that residual within each year, 2006–2022, so the map is comparable across time.
  • The Great Decoupling set = countries whose healthy-life-expectancy trend exceeded +1 yr/decade while their happiness trend was negative (linear fit over ≥6 observed years). 32 countries qualify.
  • Fully reproducible: notebooks/build_dataset.py and notebooks/build_panel.py.
